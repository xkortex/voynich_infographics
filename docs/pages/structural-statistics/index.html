Coverage is occurrence-weighted: every occurrence of a repeated triple contributes, including its first occurrence. Windows may overlap. Prefix/suffix mappings retain shorter words and use Unicode codepoints. One seeded within-document shuffle is a collision baseline, not a significance test.

Reproduce and interpret

The choices behind the pictures.

Sampling, boundaries, and estimators

Use the first up to 32,000 normalized words per language in source order. Pair sequences are capped at 200,000 units per view; selection details show actual sizes. Documents are never joined: WMF articles and VMS folios are separate sequences. VMS includes readable ZL3b paragraph loci, omitting labels and uncertain words. Omitted words close gaps: these are sequences in the cleaned text, not assertions of original adjacency.

WMF uses its text field, lowercase then NFC, retaining letters and attached combining marks. Other material splits tokens. This is an operational tokenizer, not language-specific segmentation: unspaced scripts need special care. Wikipedia and manuscript paragraphs are not genre-matched. Some language samples are shorter than the limit.

h₂ is the unsmoothed plug-in estimate −Σ p(ab) log₂[count(ab)/outgoing(a)]. Spaces count as one symbol by default. Removing them joins words within documents. The inventory includes all observed symbols, including rare loanword characters. Script, sample size, representation, and sparse pairs affect estimates. The paper’s original data and preprocessing differ.

What this says about encoding

The character views describe the same local constraint at different resolutions. The trigram view asks about sequences of whole words. Low character entropy alone does not establish a cipher, meaningful language, or meaningless text.

An injective deterministic mapping of word forms that preserves boundaries also preserves exact word-trigram recurrence. Prefix/suffix mappings are non-injective: they merge forms and generate collisions even in shuffled text. Increased coverage is not a recovered grammar or decipherment.
