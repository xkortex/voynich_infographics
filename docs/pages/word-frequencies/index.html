What exactly is being compared?

Counts and abundance. In the rank curve, count is the number of source-word tokens assigned to a family; abundance is that count divided by all included source tokens. In the frequency-of-frequencies plot, count is the number of families occurring exactly k times; relative abundance is the percentage of all families in that frequency class. The family table always reports token abundance. Ranks are assigned separately within each series, with lexical tie breaks.

Population and baseline. “No transformation” means the parsed whole-word identity is preserved, not that the file headers and layout are counted. IVTFF periods, commas (uncertain spaces), whitespace and drawing intrusions are boundaries. Other angle-bracket metadata are removed. The default population rejects entire words containing ambiguity, extended codes or ligature markup. Switch to all parsed tokens to retain those spellings literally; treatments leave non-basic words unchanged. No alternative reading is guessed and no uncertain word is split into invented readable fragments.

Morfessor families. Morfessor Baseline 2.0.6 is trained separately on each source’s basic-EVA word counts, with seed 42, recursive training and corpus weights α = 0.1, 1, 10. The family key is the longest decoded segment (leftmost on a tie), matching our earlier experiment. These are statistical groups, not established linguistic stems. We vary the lexicon/corpus trade-off, not a measured “linguistic strength”. Segmentations are saved alongside the mappings.

Rare-word collapse. Only words with original count ≤ 1 or ≤ 2 can move. A target must have an original count above that threshold and be exactly one Levenshtein insertion, deletion or substitution away. Choose the most frequent target, then lexical order on a tie. Unmatched words stay separate; there is no chaining or merging of common words. Edit similarity is not evidence of a scribal error.

Prefix, suffix and middle. Prefix/suffix-n keeps the first/last n letters, retaining shorter words intact. Middle-n removes n letters from each end. Words with length ≤ 2n retain distinct “too short” identities, rather than being pooled into one empty word. All methods conserve token mass, so a tighter vocabulary is partly a consequence of the transformation itself.

Sources and comparability. This first comparison includes the four EVA-based resources in the Voynich.nu transliteration table: ZL3b, IT2a, VT0e and RF1b basic EVA. RF is a combined reference; IT and VT share Takahashi ancestry, so these are not four independent readings. Non-EVA FG, CD and GC are omitted: applying character truncations to their different alphabets would change the units. Source coverage and uncertain-word rates also differ; this is a full-source comparison, not an aligned common-locus experiment.

Voynichese links. Links use the original complete EVA word and an observed folio, never a truncated family key. Voynichese highlights exact-spelling matches on that folio, not a unique occurrence. A different transcription’s spelling may not exist in its text; those links are candidates, clearly labelled. Marked tokens get a folio link only. Foldout folio identifiers are retained from the source.
